The Appeal

Appeal of ordinary people to the United Nations for the Rule of Law

An abstract — The Hague, 15 September 2026

In this Appeal we, ordinary people, put forward a social demand for justice concerning the disruptive conduct of the State of Israel in the Middle East. Expressed in many languages, it is supported by people from around the world.

Through this Appeal to the Secretary General, we call for respect of fundamental values and the Rule of Law, as many common citizens have often expressed and continue to plead worldwide. We, ordinary people, do not profess expertise. Nonetheless, we use precise language to explain what deeply troubles us.

The many forms of violence perpetrated by the State of Israel and its failure to respect the Rule of Law in the Palestinian and Syrian territories it has occupied since 1967, as well as in Lebanon and elsewhere in the region, have caused immense suffering. They are therefore at the heart of this Appeal to the United Nations which accompanied the very creation of the State of Israel.

Nothing in this Appeal is intended to deny the State of Israel’s sovereignty or right to exist. We, ordinary people, consider the conduct of the State of Israel unacceptable. We are convinced that adherence to the Rule of Law is essential to achieving lasting peace between the States of Israel and Palestine and ensuring a constructive future for all their inhabitants.

Five demands. One Appeal.

  • Suspend the full membership of the State of Israel at the UN and in all UN agencies, limiting it to observer status.
  • Compel Israel to withdraw from the occupied territories, in accordance with UN Security Council Resolution 242, the ruling of the International Court of Justice of 19 July 2024 and UN General Assembly Resolution 194.
  • Act in such a manner that the State of Israel respects the Rule of Law.
  • Reinvent the United Nations so that its agencies are protected, Human Rights are defended, and the General Assembly can act promptly and collectively in cases of genocide and war crimes, if necessary, circumventing the Security Council.
  • Systematically involve women in a meaningful way in decision-making processes and agencies.
